Detection and ranking are separate jobs. The graph pass is built for recall and finds nearly every ring, but raises tens of thousands of ordinary account clusters alongside them — because most groups of people who transact together are just people who transact together. Ranking is what makes the output something a team could actually work.

Latency — three questions, three answers

The BFSI literature sets a sub-100ms bar for fraud decisioning. That bar was written for per-transaction scoring, and WARREN's unit of decision is a group of accounts over a 72-hour window. Quoting only the number that clears it would be the flattering framing, so all three are here.

Scoring clears the 100ms bar by roughly six orders of magnitude, and the floor row is there so that claim can be checked: the reading is several times the cost of taking it, not the clock measuring itself. Arrival to decision does not clear the bar and cannot, because the evidence for a ring does not exist until the window closes. The ceiling is the 24-hour stride between window closures, and the measurement lands exactly on it, which is the check that the number is the architecture rather than an accident. That wait is the honest cost of detecting the ring instead of the payment, and what it buys is a class of loss a 10ms per-transaction scorer is structurally unable to see at all.
