Holds in force

Every account whose money this system is currently sitting on, how long for, and the decision that authorised it. A hold whose authority cannot be named is one WARREN will not place: the lease is written only after the decision is in the audit chain, so if the record fails, nothing is held.

Two geometries, one of which may act

WARREN runs detection twice. The {{.Analyst}} pass feeds the alert queue a person works; it never leases, whatever it decides. The {{.Enforcer}} pass is the only one with authority to place a hold. Narrow windows decide sooner, and deciding sooner is the whole of what enforcement can buy — an oracle detector told the true ring membership could stop 10.76% of ring value at 72-hour windows and 31.75% at 24-hour ones. Letting the wide pass lease as well was measured at +8.4% more innocent money held for +1.9% of the laundering value, which is the wrong side of that trade. So the gates already on blocking are the only gates on enforcement; there is no second, looser path to freezing an account.

Every high-scoring ring in this ledger moves far more than the autonomous ceiling, so on this queue the freeze path is unreachable by design rather than unbuilt. The watches below are what the system did instead: recorded, visible, expiring, and touching nobody's money.

Lifting is a first-class operation, not an administrative correction: a bounded action that cannot be undone before it expires is not bounded, it is just slow. A lift is appended to the ledger rather than deleting the hold, because "this account was frozen and then released" is the sentence an audit needs, and a table of current state cannot say it. Lifts carry no decision number — a release performed by a person is authorised by that person, and attributing it to a machine's decision would be a lie about who decided.
